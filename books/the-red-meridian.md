    <p>Cal Calloway reads rock for a living. He knows what holds and what doesn't, what bears weight and what will eventually fail. He's never spoken to a room larger than a shift crew, never stood in front of a delegate from anywhere, never wanted to. Then he says one sentence at a rally, and nothing stays the same.</p>

    <p>The delegates assembling in convention range from committed to cautious to openly hostile. The constitutional language has to hold not just for the signing, but for the generations that will test it. Earth's Colonial Administration is watching, maneuvering, using every legal and economic instrument at its disposal to make the project fail before it becomes real. And somewhere in the inner circle Cal has trusted with everything, someone is feeding the Administration exactly what it needs.</p>

    <p>Jack Masters, who does not say things he doesn't mean, saw what Cal was from across a crowded commons and named it before Cal had a word for it himself. That recognition carries weight — and responsibility. Because the quality that makes Cal good at his work and the quality that made him blind to the truth sitting beside him on a bench for sixteen years turn out to be the same quality. Knowing that changes nothing and everything at once.</p>

    <p><em>The Red Meridian</em> is a story about what it costs to build something from nothing — the political architecture, the personal faith, the willingness to put your name on a document knowing history will have its way with it. It is about loyalty and what happens when the person you would have sworn for turns out to be the one who broke the line.</p>
